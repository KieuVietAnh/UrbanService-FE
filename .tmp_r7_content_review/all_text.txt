2. Product Background
Urban areas are facing an increasing number of environmental and public service issues such as illegal waste disposal, damaged infrastructure, drainage problems, public lighting failures and unsafe public spaces. Residents often do not know which unit is responsible for a particular issue and have to rely on hotlines, social media posts or informal communication channels, so many reports are lost, duplicated or answered late.
On the operating side, the unit that receives these reports has to read free-text descriptions, identify the location, decide which area is responsible, assign a handler, coordinate with external service providers and answer the resident. Without a single system that keeps the whole history in one place, the result is a slow response, repeated work on the same problem and poor visibility of processing progress.
UrbanMind is proposed as an AI-powered Urban Service Interaction and Feedback Ecosystem that connects residents, system staff, interaction managers, service provider coordinators and administrators. A resident can send a report from the web application, the Android application or a Facebook Messenger conversation. Each report is screened by staff, grouped into an incident, routed to the responsible operating area and tracked against a service-level target until it is resolved and reviewed.
The system uses AI as an assistant, not as a decision maker. A self-hosted vision-language model reads the description together with the attached images and suggests a service category, an operating area, a sentiment, an urgency and a severity level, produces a short summary and proposes possible duplicates. Staff review every suggestion and can correct it before the report is processed officially.
Beyond individual reports, the platform publishes a community feed and map of verified incidents, so that residents can follow, comment on and support the issues in their neighbourhood, subscribe to the areas they care about and receive an alert when something important happens there.


3. Existing Systems

3.1 Hue-S
Hue-S is a smart urban platform developed in Thua Thien Hue, Viet Nam, which includes a field feedback service for receiving and processing citizen reports. Since 2018 the platform has been used to collect feedback, opinions and concerns from residents and to route them to the relevant government authority for handling.
The system acts as a bridge between citizens and local government, ensuring that urban issues are reported, tracked and resolved. Hue-S is a practical reference for the lifecycle of a citizen report and for the way processing progress is published back to the reporter.
Main actors
Citizen: submits field feedback or incident reports.
Receiving staff: screens incoming reports and forwards them to the responsible unit.
Handling agency: resolves the issues assigned to it.
Supervising unit: monitors processing progress and response quality.
Administrator: manages the platform and its configuration.
Key Features
Submit field feedback: citizens report urban issues such as infrastructure damage, environmental problems or public service concerns.
Track feedback status: citizens follow the progress of their submitted reports.
Public feedback information: selected reports and violations are displayed publicly for transparency.
Government-side processing: authorities receive, assign and handle the reported issues.
Pros
Real-world public-sector urban feedback model.
Strong support for citizen-to-government interaction.
Proven practical deployment in Viet Nam.
Provides a clear reference for the feedback lifecycle and for status monitoring.
Cons / Limitations
Primarily focused on basic feedback intake and processing.
Limited support for interaction history and multi-turn communication tracking.
No AI-assisted classification, summarisation or severity suggestion.
No automatic duplicate detection or grouping of related reports.
Limited service-level monitoring of response and resolution deadlines.
Limited coordination workflow with external service providers.
Limited community interaction around the published reports.
3.2 FixMyStreet
FixMyStreet is an open-source platform developed by mySociety in the United Kingdom that lets citizens report local street and public-space problems such as potholes, broken street lighting, graffiti or fly-tipping. A report is created by pinning its exact position on a map, adding a description and attaching photos, and the platform forwards it to the authority responsible for that location.
All reports are published on a public map, so anyone can see what has been reported nearby and whether it has been fixed. FixMyStreet is a useful reference for map-centred reporting and for publishing citizen reports transparently.
Main Actors
Citizen: creates a report by selecting its location on the map and follows the response.
Local authority staff: receive the reports for their area and update the outcome.
Public viewer: browses the reported and fixed problems on the public map.
Administrator: manages categories, authority contact data and moderation.
Key Features
Map-based reporting: the reporter pins the exact position of the problem instead of typing an address.
Automatic routing of a report to the authority responsible for the reported location.
Public report map: every report and its status are visible to everyone.
Email updates: the reporter is notified when the status of the report changes.
Open source and open data: the platform and the report data are openly available.
Pros
Simple and proven citizen reporting flow centred on the map.
Strong transparency: every report is public by default.
Automatic routing of a report to the authority responsible for the location.
Open-source implementation that can be studied and reused.
Low barrier of entry for residents; no complex form is required.
Cons / Limitations
No AI-assisted classification, summarisation or severity suggestion.
No automatic duplicate detection or merging of related reports.
No service-level agreement tracking with response and resolution deadlines.
No internal assignment workflow between staff, managers and service providers.
No coordination or contract management with external service providers.
Intake is limited to the web channel; there is no chatbot intake through messaging platforms.
No conversational channel between the reporter and the handling staff inside the platform.
Analytics are limited; there is no operational dashboard for sentiment or hotspot monitoring.

3.3 Freshdesk
Freshdesk is a cloud-based customer support and ticket management platform developed by Freshworks. The system helps organizations receive, manage, assign, and track customer requests from multiple communication channels in a centralized environment.
Although Freshdesk is not specifically designed for urban service management, it provides many useful concepts for ticket lifecycle management, customer interaction tracking, and service quality monitoring.
Main Actors
Customer: submits a support request through one of the supported channels.
Support agent: receives and resolves the assigned tickets.
Team supervisor: monitors workload, response time and service quality.
System administrator: manages the account, the workflow rules and the configuration.
Key Features
Multi-channel Ticket Collection
The platform receives requests from several channels, including email, web forms, live chat, social media and messaging platforms.
Ticket Assignment & Workflow
Tickets are assigned automatically to the appropriate team or agent according to predefined business rules and workflows.
Conversation Management
All exchanges between the customer and the support agents are stored inside a single ticket, which keeps the complete communication history.
Analytics & Reporting
Managers monitor ticket volume, response time, resolution rate and customer satisfaction through dashboards and reports.




Pros
Mature ticket management workflow.
Strong interaction and communication tracking inside a ticket.
Multi-channel intake, including email, chat and messaging platforms.
Rich reporting and analytics features.
Scales to a large number of requests.

Cons / Limitations
Not designed specifically for urban service operations.
No map or location-based incident management.
No built-in urban issue categorisation or area-based responsibility model.
No AI-driven duplicate detection or merging of related reports.
No coordination with external service providers, and no public community view of the reports.

	None of the existing systems combines multi-channel citizen reporting, AI-assisted analysis, duplicate detection, area-based routing with service-level monitoring, coordination with external service providers and community transparency inside one platform. The proposed system aims to close this gap by integrating these capabilities into a single urban service ecosystem.
	
4. Business Opportunity
The number of urban service reports keeps growing in residential areas, commercial zones and new urban developments. Residents struggle to identify the unit responsible for an issue, while the units that receive the reports lack an efficient platform to screen, group, assign, monitor and answer them.
UrbanMind addresses this gap by operating as an AI-assisted urban service coordination platform that connects residents, system staff, interaction managers, service provider coordinators and administrators. The platform supports two primary operations:
Citizen reporting and coordination: residents report urban issues from the web application, the mobile application or a Facebook Messenger conversation. AI suggests the category, the area, the severity and possible duplicates; staff verify the report, group it into an incident and route it to the responsible area and handler under a service-level target.
Service provider collaboration: an incident that cannot be handled internally is routed to a provider coordinator working under a managed contract. The coordinator records contact logs, execution progress and completion documents, and the result is reviewed and approved before the incident is closed.
The platform creates value through:
AI-assisted intake, classification and summarisation of citizen reports.
Duplicate detection that prevents the same problem from being handled several times.
Area-based routing and service-level monitoring of every incident.
Coordinated collaboration with external service providers under managed contracts.
Realtime notification, area alerts and continuous communication with residents.
Operational analytics on volume, status, SLA compliance, sentiment and location hotspots.
This approach allows UrbanMind to operate not only as a reporting channel but as an urban service coordination ecosystem that improves response time, reduces duplicated work and makes the handling of public issues transparent to the residents who reported them.


5. Software Product Vision
For residents and organisations that need to report an urban issue and follow its handling, UrbanMind is an AI-assisted service interaction platform that lets them submit a report from the channel they already use, communicate with the staff in charge and track processing progress in real time.
Unlike traditional hotlines or standalone reporting applications, UrbanMind combines multi-channel intake, AI-assisted classification and duplicate detection, area-based assignment, service-level monitoring, service provider coordination and community transparency inside a single ecosystem.
The platform helps operating units respond faster, avoid repeated work on the same problem, keep a complete and traceable history of every report and give residents a clear, verifiable answer. By connecting all stakeholders through one platform, the Urban Service Interaction and Feedback Ecosystem contributes to more effective urban service management and smarter city operations.

6. Project Scope & Limitations
6.1 Major Features
6.1.1. Feedback Intake & Data Processing
FE-01: Multi-channel Feedback Submission & Validation
Allows a resident to submit an urban feedback report from the web application, the Android application or a Facebook Messenger conversation. The system validates the submitted information, including title, description, location text, map coordinates, category and supporting evidence, before the report is accepted.
FE-02: Media Upload & Storage
Enables residents and staff to attach images as supporting evidence for a report. Files are uploaded to a cloud media service, and the number and the size of the files accepted from each channel are limited by configuration.
FE-03: Account Verification & Submission Control
Requires a resident account to have a verified email address before a report can be submitted from the web or mobile application. Verification uses a six-digit one-time password sent by email. An account created through Google login is verified automatically when Google has already verified the address. An unverified account can still sign in, follow incidents and receive notifications, but it cannot submit a new report until email verification is completed. This rule raises the cost of creating throwaway accounts and reduces spam submissions.

6.1.2. AI-powered Processing
FE-04: AI Understanding & Classification
Applies a self-hosted vision-language model to the description and the attached images of a submitted report. The model suggests the service category, the operating area, the sentiment, the urgency and the severity level, produces a short summary and extracts keywords together with a confidence score. Every suggestion is stored as an analysis result that staff can review and correct.
FE-05: Duplicate Detection & Incident Consolidation
Identifies reports that describe the same problem by comparing content similarity, map distance and reporting time within a configurable radius and look-back window. Candidates are proposed to staff, who confirm or reject them. Confirmed reports are linked to a single incident, and related incidents can be merged so that one problem is handled once.
FE-06: AI Urban Service Chatbot & Draft Assistant
Provides a conversational assistant that answers resident questions about urban services and guides them through the reporting process using a knowledge base about the platform. The same assistant can turn a free-text message and attached photos into a structured feedback draft that the resident confirms before sending.

6.1.3. Incident Handling, Assignment & SLA Management
FE-07: Feedback & Incident Lifecycle Management
Supports the full lifecycle of a citizen report and of the incident it belongs to, including creation, viewing, editing, verification, approval, status change, rework request, merging and closing. Every change is recorded in an incident timeline that can be replayed.
FE-08: Assignment & Area-based Routing
Routes an incident to the staff responsible for its operating area and lets a manager assign it to a specific handler. Responsibility is configured through staff area assignments, manager area assignments and provider coverage, so each account only sees and acts on the areas it is responsible for.
FE-09: SLA Management
Monitors service-level targets for the first response and for the resolution, based on configurable SLA policies. The system supports starting, responding, pausing, resuming, recalculating, completing and cancelling an SLA, and a background worker checks running SLAs periodically to raise a warning before a deadline and to record a breach.
FE-10: Resolution Recording, Approval & Rework
Lets staff record the resolution of an incident together with its evidence, and lets an interaction manager approve the result or send it back for rework. The approval history and the reason for each rework request are kept with the incident.

6.1.4. Service Provider Coordination
FE-11: Service Provider & Coordinator Management
Manages the service provider coordinators that the operating unit works with, including their contact information, their active state and the operating areas and service categories they cover.
FE-12: Provider Contract Management
Manages the contracts signed with service providers, including the contract period, its scope and its supporting attachments, so that every assignment can be traced back to a valid contract.
FE-13: Provider Assignment & Execution Reporting
Assigns an incident to a suitable provider coordinator, tracks the status of that assignment and records contact logs describing each exchange with the provider during execution.
FE-14: Completion Evidence Management
Stores the completion documents and images uploaded for a provider assignment, so that the reported result can be verified before the incident is approved and closed.

6.1.5. Community, Communication & Notification
FE-15: Public Incident Feed & Map
Publishes verified incidents on a public feed and map, so that residents can see what has been reported in their neighbourhood, open the detail of an incident and follow its timeline.
FE-16: Community Comments & Support
Allows residents to comment on a published incident and to support it, so that the operating unit can see how many people are affected by the same problem. A resident can edit or delete their own comment.
FE-17: Incident Subscription & Area Alerts
Lets residents subscribe to an incident or to an operating area. Staff can publish an area alert from an incident, and every subscriber of that area receives it.
FE-18: Citizen Resolution Review
Lets the resident who reported a problem review the resolution after the incident has been handled, so that the operating unit receives feedback on the quality of the result.
FE-19: Interaction Messaging
Stores and maintains the conversation between a resident and the system staff handling their report, including requests for additional information and the answers given to them.
FE-20: Notification System
Sends a notification when a relevant event occurs, such as a status change, an assignment, a request for additional information, an SLA warning, an approval, an area alert or a new community interaction. Notifications are delivered in real time over SignalR and kept in a notification centre, and important events are also sent by email.

6.1.6. Monitoring, Analytics & Administration
FE-21: Dashboard & KPI Monitoring
Provides operational dashboards showing incident volume, status, priority, category and area distribution, the figures of the current day, the monthly trend, urgent open incidents and recent activity, scoped by the role and the operating areas of the signed-in account.
FE-22: SLA, Sentiment & Hotspot Analytics
Provides analytics on SLA compliance and performance, on the incidents that are nearing or past their deadline, on resident sentiment trends derived from the AI analysis, and on location hotspots shown on a heat map.
FE-23: User & Role Management
Manages accounts and access permissions for the five roles of the system, namely Service User, System Staff, Interaction Manager, Service Operator Staff and System Administrator, including account creation, update, activation and password reset.
FE-24: Master Data Administration
Manages the reference data that the workflow depends on, including operating areas, urban service categories, SLA policies, staff and manager area assignments, and provider coverage.
FE-25: Audit Trail & System Monitoring
Records system activity, incident events, status history and integration errors, and exposes a health endpoint and the status of the AI review queue, so that the team can trace what happened to a report and monitor background processing.




6.2 Limitations & Exclusions

Limitation ID | Limitation / Exclusion
LI-01 | The platform coordinates the handling of citizen reports. It does not perform the field work itself, and some cases must still be transferred to external organisations outside the system.
LI-02 | AI results are suggestions only. Staff review and may modify the suggested category, area, severity and duplicate candidates before a report is processed officially.
LI-03 | The AI features are built on a small self-hosted vision-language model driven by prompts, not on a fine-tuned model. Accuracy is therefore limited, in particular for short descriptions or low-quality images, and no labelled training dataset is produced in this project.
LI-04 | Duplicate detection may not be fully accurate because the description, the map position and the reporting time supplied by a resident can be incomplete or incorrect. Confirmation by staff is always required.
LI-05 | The platform is designed for urban service coordination and operational feedback management. It is not intended to replace official legal complaint, law enforcement or governmental administrative systems.
LI-06 | Location data is stored as latitude and longitude, and distance is computed without a spatial database extension. Advanced geospatial analysis and prediction are not included in this project.
LI-07 | The mobile application is delivered for Android only and focuses on resident functions together with a limited staff workspace. Administrative and operational management functions are provided through the web portals.
LI-08 | Intake through Facebook Messenger depends on an approved Page and on the quotas of that platform. The same applies to the email and media services used by the system, which run on free or trial plans during the project.
LI-09 | The system is deployed on a single virtual private server without redundancy, so availability and real-time processing depend on the capacity of that server and of the third-party services it calls.
LI-10 | Online payment and paid service booking are out of scope. The platform does not process any financial transaction and does not operate as a commercial service marketplace.
LI-11 | The project originally intended to verify a resident by phone number, because a SIM in Viet Nam is registered against a national identity document, which would allow an abusive account to be traced and banned. Sending SMS from an application requires a registered brand name, which the project could not obtain, so verification was changed to email. Email verification does not identify a person, so a determined user can still create several accounts. Spam is therefore mitigated by staff verification of every report before it becomes an incident, by duplicate detection and by the ability of an administrator to deactivate an account, not by the account itself.

	Overall Project Objective
	The objective of this project is to design and develop UrbanMind, an AI-assisted Urban Service Interaction and Feedback Ecosystem that connects residents, system staff, interaction managers, service provider coordinators and administrators through a unified digital platform.
	The platform supports the complete lifecycle of a citizen report, from multi-channel intake and AI-assisted analysis to verification, incident consolidation, area-based assignment, service-level monitoring, coordination with external service providers, resolution approval and community follow-up.
	The project focuses on:
	Providing a centralized platform for urban issue reporting over the web, the mobile application and the Facebook Messenger channel.
	Improving operational efficiency through AI-assisted classification, duplicate detection, chatbot assistance, area-based routing and SLA monitoring.
	Making every report traceable through an incident timeline, a status history and an audit trail.
	Enabling collaboration between the internal operating unit and external service provider coordinators under managed contracts.
	Increasing transparency for residents through a public incident feed, community interaction, area alerts and resolution review.
	Delivering a scalable and maintainable software solution within the available project resources and timeline.

III. Software Requirement Specification
1. Product Overview
UrbanMind is an urban report and incident coordination system. Residents submit location-based reports from web, mobile and Messenger, attach evidence, follow public or subscribed incidents, comment, support an incident and review approved outcomes. AI reviews each report and proposes matching candidates, while authorized managers verify reports and create or link them to an Incident. The Incident then owns staff assignment, provider coordination, SLA, resolution review and operational dashboards. The backend is an ASP.NET Core Web API with PostgreSQL, JWT, SignalR, Cloudinary, Brevo email, Meta Messenger and configurable AI providers.

3.2 Common Functions
3.2.1 Sign in and refresh session
Function trigger: A user submits email/password credentials or the client needs to renew an authenticated session.
Actor: Public Visitor; all authenticated roles for token refresh.
Function description: Authenticates an active account, returns the phone-verification flag and maintains the JWT access/refresh-token session. Unverified residents can sign in but cannot submit reports.
Screen layout:

Function details:
User enters email and password and selects Sign in.
The system validates required fields, account active state, email verification and password hash.
The system returns an access token, refresh token and role-aware account data on success.
When the access token expires, the client submits the stored refresh token.
The system rejects expired, revoked or mismatched refresh tokens and otherwise renews the session.
This function is supported by the web application or the named integration channel.
Business Rules: BR-006, BR-007, BR-012, BR-013, BR-021, BR-032.
3.2.2 Register and verify email
Function trigger: A public visitor creates a resident account and completes email verification.
Actor: Public Visitor; Service User.
Function description: Creates a SERVICEUSER account, sends a cooldown-controlled OTP and verifies the email address.
Screen layout:

Function details:

Registration creates the account with an unverified email address and the email provider sends a six-digit OTP to that address. The phone number is optional at registration.
The resident may request resend after the configured cooldown; only a hashed OTP is cached.
A correct current OTP marks the phone verified and returns refreshed access/refresh tokens.
This function is supported by the web application or the named integration channel.

4. Non-Functional Requirements
4.1 External Interfaces
The backend communicates with clients through REST APIs, Swagger/OpenAPI and the SignalR notification hub. It integrates with PostgreSQL through EF Core/Npgsql, Cloudinary for media, Brevo for email/OTP,
Ollama/OpenRouter/Gemini-compatible AI clients for analysis and chat, Meta Messenger webhooks for channel feedback intake.
4.2 Quality Attributes
The following quality attributes are required for the current UrbanMind backend and clientfacing workflows.
4.2.1 Usability
The system shall provide clear validation messages, consistent API responses, searchable/paged lists, and role-appropriate screens for residents, staff, interaction managers and administrators. Common workflows such as submit feedback, verify feedback, assign provider coordinator, update SLA and review resolution should be discoverable without technical knowledge.
4.2.2 Reliability
The system shall preserve feedback data, attachments metadata, status history, SLA events, contact logs and notifications even when external services temporarily fail.
Availability target: core APIs should be deployable as a Dockerized service and expose /health for monitoring.
Background workers for AI review, Messenger webhook processing and SLA monitoring should recover after application restart and continue processing queued or persisted data.
Database transactions should protect multi-step workflows such as assignment, status change and resolution submission.
Notifications should be stored in the database before or alongside realtime SignalR delivery so users can still read them later.
External integration failures should return controlled error responses and must not corrupt feedback workflow state.
Only authorized roles may update feedback status, SLA data, provider reports, users, areas, categories and provider configuration.
4.2.3 Performance
The system should return common list/detail API responses within normal interactive web expectations under typical load. Paging is required for feedback lists, dashboards, notifications, provider lists and management screens.
AI analysis may run asynchronously so resident feedback submission is not blocked by longrunning model calls.
Media upload size and file count should follow configured Cloudinary/API limits.
SLA monitoring interval, warning threshold and initial delay should be configurable by environment variables.
SignalR notification delivery should not block the database status update transaction.
4.2.4 Security
All protected APIs shall require JWT bearer authentication and role authorization at the controller boundary.
Access tokens shall include a valid user ID and role; refresh tokens shall be revocable and expirationcontrolled.
Inactive accounts shall be denied authentication and protected operations.
Residents shall access only their own profile, private feedback, conversations, notifications, subscriptions and resolution reviews.
Management operations shall follow the implemented role matrix; provider coordinators shall not be treated as authenticated web actors.
Messenger webhook signatures and idempotency data shall be validated before business processing.
Passwords shall be stored as hashes; provider and integration tokens shall be encrypted or kept in secret configuration.
Client errors shall not expose stack traces, secrets, connection strings, provider tokens or raw infrastructure details.
Feedback status, SLA events, provider contacts, duplicate decisions and audit logs shall support traceability of privileged actions.

1. System Design
1.1 System Architecture

No | Name | Description
1 | Browser | A browser is an application program that provides a way to look at and interact with all the information on the World Wide Web. This includes Web pages, videos and images.
2 | Mobile | A mobile application allows users to access and interact with the system through mobile devices. It displays data, receives user input, and sends requests to the front-end or back-end services through the internet.
3 | Front-end | The component displays data and receives user interaction. It is responsible for sending a request to the back-end to get the data and display it in the browser.
4 | Back-end | The component receives the request from the web application. It handles business logic, database queries, and external systems. Then return data as a response to the web.
5 | Database | The component stores the data of the system.
6 | Cloud storage & messaging | Cloud storage is an online data storage service, allowing users to store and access information from anywhere via the internet. Instead of storing data on personal devices, users can store files, images and other data on remotely managed servers. Cloud storage offers high flexibility, easy and secure scalability, with automatic backups and data security through strong measures such as encryption. This helps optimize data management and reduce the risk of information loss due to device damage or loss.

17 | Shared Types | Defines common data types or interfaces used across the front-end application, such as User, Feedback, Service, Incident, or ApiResponse.

A. User Registration
Description
The registration function allows a new user to create an UrbanMind account by providing the required personal and authentication information. After the account is created successfully, the user must verify the registered email address before submitting feedback or using functions that require a verified account.
Step 1: Open the Registration Page
From the Login page, select the registration option to open the account registration form.

Step 2: Enter Registration Information
Enter the required information in the registration form, including:
Full name

Password
Password confirmation
The system validates the entered information before allowing the registration request to be submitted.

Figure 3.2.2. New account registration information
Step 3: Submit the Registration Request
After completing all required fields, submit the registration form.
The system validates the provided information and creates a new user account if the information is valid.
If invalid or duplicate information is detected, the system displays the corresponding validation message.
Step 4: Verify the Email Address
After successful registration, the system sends a six-digit OTP to the registered email address.
Enter the received OTP on the email verification screen.

Figure 3.2.3. Email verification screen
Step 5: Complete Account Verification
Submit the verification code.
If the verification is successful, the email address is marked as verified and the user can continue to functions that require a verified account.
